## Requirements Discrepancies

### 1. Idaho (ID) — registrationDeadline

- **Current value:** "25 days before election by mail; 11 days before online (same-day registration available on election day)"
- **Correct value:** "11 days before election by mail and online (same-day registration available on election day)"
- **Sources:**
  - VoteIdaho.gov (Tier 1) — https://voteidaho.gov/voter-registration/
  - NPR voter registration guide (Tier 3) — https://apps.npr.org/voter-registration-2026-mail/idaho.html
  - VOTE411 (Tier 3) — https://www.vote411.org/idaho
  - Rock the Vote (Tier 3) — https://www.rockthevote.org/how-to-vote/idaho/
  - Ballotpedia (Tier 3) — https://ballotpedia.org/Voting_in_Idaho
- **Notes:** Five independent sources all confirm the mail registration deadline is 11 days before election (received by 5:00 PM), the same as the online deadline. The "25 days" figure does not appear on any current authoritative source.

### 2. Kentucky (KY) — felonyVotingRules

- **Current value:** "Rights not automatically restored. Must apply for restoration from the governor after completion of sentence."
- **Correct value:** "Non-violent felonies: rights automatically restored upon completion of sentence (per Governor Beshear's 2019 Executive Order). Violent crimes, treason, bribery, and sex offenses: must apply for restoration from the governor."
- **Sources:**
  - Election Protection / 866ourvote.org (Tier 3) — https://866ourvote.org/state/kentucky/
  - Kentucky Lantern (Tier 3) — https://kentuckylantern.com/2026/02/18/ky-senate-committee-backs-bill-to-restore-voting-rights-to-some-with-felony-convictions/
  - Brennan Center for Justice (Tier 3) — https://www.brennancenter.org/press-release/kentucky-governor-creates-new-process-help-restore-voting-rights-170000-citizens
- **Notes:** Governor Beshear's December 12, 2019 Executive Order automatically restores voting rights for Kentuckians convicted of non-violent felonies who have completed their sentence. Three corroborating Tier 3 sources confirm this.

### 3. Louisiana (LA) — felonyVotingRules

- **Current value:** "Rights restored 5 years after completion of sentence (incarceration, probation, and parole) without a new felony conviction."
- **Correct value:** "Rights restored if not incarcerated for a felony within the last 5 years, even while still under an order of imprisonment (on probation or parole). Those who have fully completed their sentence have rights automatically restored. People convicted of election fraud felonies are permanently disenfranchised."
- **Sources:**
  - Louisiana Secretary of State — Register to Vote (Tier 1) — https://www.sos.la.gov/ElectionsAndVoting/RegisterToVote/
  - Sentencing Project — Locked Out 2024 (Tier 3) — https://www.sentencingproject.org/reports/locked-out-2024-four-million-denied-voting-rights-due-to-a-felony-conviction/
- **Notes:** The current wording implies rights are restored only after the entire sentence (including probation and parole) is complete plus 5 years. The actual law: people on probation or parole can vote as long as they haven't been incarcerated in the last 5 years. The 5-year clock runs from last incarceration, not from end of sentence.

### 4. Maine (ME) — eligibilityAge

- **Current value:** "18 (may register at 17 if turning 18 by election day)"
- **Correct value:** "18 (may pre-register at 16)"
- **Sources:**
  - Maine Secretary of State — Registering to Vote (Tier 1) — https://www.maine.gov/sos/elections-voting/registering-to-vote
  - Ballotpedia — Voting in Maine (Tier 3) — https://ballotpedia.org/Voting_in_Maine
- **Notes:** Since January 1, 2020, Maine has allowed pre-registration at age 16. The current data only mentions 17-year-olds registering if turning 18 by election day, which is incomplete.

### 5. Massachusetts (MA) — pendingLegislation (H.834/S.505 status)

- **Current value:** Status listed as "S.505 referred to Senate Committee on Ways and Means"
- **Correct value:** "S.505 reported favorably by Election Laws Committee (Nov 2025); legislature did not vote on bill. Same-day registration now advancing as ballot initiative (H.5001) for November 2026 ballot; legislature has until first Wednesday of May 2026 to act before it goes to voters."
- **Sources:**
  - Ballotpedia — Massachusetts Permit Same-Day Voter Registration Initiative (2026) (Tier 3) — https://ballotpedia.org/Massachusetts_Permit_Same-Day_Voter_Registration_Initiative_(2026)
  - Massachusetts Legislature — Bill S.505 (Tier 3) — https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/S505
- **Notes:** The current status is outdated. The bill progressed past committee but the legislature never voted on it. The same-day registration concept has moved to the ballot initiative track (H.5001, 87,408 valid signatures certified Dec 18, 2025).

### 6. Oklahoma (OK) — felonyVotingRules

- **Current value:** "Rights restored upon completion of full sentence, or after any period of incarceration if not a repeat offender."
- **Correct value:** "Rights restored upon completion of full sentence, including any term of incarceration, parole, or supervision. Also restored immediately upon pardon or commutation."
- **Sources:**
  - Oklahoma Official Elections — Register to Vote (Tier 1) — https://oklahoma.gov/elections/voter-registration/register-to-vote.html
  - Ballotpedia — Voting in Oklahoma (Tier 3) — https://ballotpedia.org/Voting_in_Oklahoma
  - US Vote Foundation — Oklahoma (Tier 3) — https://www.usvotefoundation.org/voting-rights-restoration/oklahoma
  - Brennan Center for Justice (Tier 3) — https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/voting-rights-restoration-efforts-oklahoma
- **Notes:** The clause "or after any period of incarceration if not a repeat offender" is not supported by any current authoritative source. All sources state rights are restored upon completion of the full sentence with no distinction between first-time and repeat offenders.
